{\Large Daily and fixed 24-hour-ahead forecasts with reconciliation and conformal intervals\par}
\vspace{1.2cm}
\begin{abstract}
This study asks how accurately German electricity demand can be forecast from a restricted
forecast-origin information set. We compare one-day-ahead daily models, fixed 24-hour-ahead hourly
forecasts, daily--hourly reconciliation, and three
forms of uncertainty quantification. Model choice is fixed on a chronological validation period and
a separate calibration period is used for interval construction. On the previously inspected final
period, the daily ensemble achieved \BestMAPE{} MAPE and \BestMASE{} MASE. Reconciliation reduced
hourly MAE from \RawHourlyMAE{} MW to \ReconciledHourlyMAE{} MW. A paired day-block bootstrap did
not distinguish the residual hybrid from direct LightGBM. Adaptive 90\% intervals reached
\AdaptiveCoverage{} empirical coverage, but this retrospective result does not provide a prospective guarantee.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Load forecasts inform scheduling and operational planning, yet a retrospective model can
appear stronger than it is if it uses weather observations that were unavailable when the forecast
would have been issued. This study treats forecast origin as part of the prediction problem. The
analysis asks two related questions: how accurately the next daily mean can be predicted, and how
accurately each valid hour can be predicted exactly 24 hours earlier.

The analysis compares daily statistical and tree models, three direct hourly designs, a constraint
that aligns hourly profiles with an independently forecast daily mean, and three interval methods.
The dashboard offers a compact tour of the evidence. This report develops the experimental
assumptions, statistical comparisons, error structure, and limitations in greater depth.

\section{Data and evaluation design}

The target is realized German electricity consumption reported by SMARD (filter 410), expressed in
MW. Hourly observations are normalized in UTC and mapped to the Europe/Berlin local calendar.
Open-Meteo supplies temperature at 2 m and wind speed at 10 m for Berlin, Hamburg, Munich, Cologne,
and Frankfurt. Population weights combine these series into a national weather proxy. It captures
broad exposure rather than regional grid heterogeneity.

The dataset contains 2,760 daily observations from 14 January 2019 through 4 August 2026. From 20
January 2024 onward, weather features use archived forecasts issued 24 hours before the target day.
Earlier dates use the previous day's realized weather because an equivalent forecast archive is
unavailable. The two ranges therefore differ in weather-information quality.

The chronological split is training through 2023, validation from January 2024 through June 2025,
calibration from July through December 2025, and final evaluation from January through 4 August
2026. Model and feature decisions use validation evidence only. Calibration outcomes set interval
widths but do not select the point model. The final period contains \FinalN{} local days and
\HourlyN{} hourly observations. Because that period was inspected during development, it is reported
as retrospective evidence rather than an untouched holdout.

Daily predictors include one- and seven-day load lags, weekly and annual Fourier terms, holidays,
weekend and bridge-day indicators, and heating and cooling degree variables. Candidate forecasts comprise SARIMAX, XGBoost,
LightGBM, Random Forest, two persistence baselines, and an ensemble of SARIMAX, XGBoost, and
LightGBM fixed from validation evidence.

For observations $y_t$ and forecasts $\hat y_t$, the main measures are
\[
\mathrm{MAE}=n^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{n}|y_t-\hat y_t|,
\qquad
\mathrm{MAPE}=100n^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\left|\frac{y_t-\hat y_t}{y_t}\right|.
\]
MASE scales absolute error by the in-sample one-step naive error; values below one improve on that
scale reference. RMSE is retained to reveal sensitivity to large errors.

The hourly evaluation compares residual hybrid, direct LightGBM, and direct Ridge. All candidates
predict valid horizons directly and use horizon, 24- and 168-hour lags, weather, calendar, and
Fourier features. Direct Ridge provides a linear reference. Direct LightGBM fits the nonlinear
relationship in one model. The hybrid first estimates a linear trend and then fits LightGBM to its
residuals. Direct prediction avoids recursive error accumulation. Reconciled validation MAE selects
the hourly model; later rankings do not revise that decision. The released hourly metrics use only
horizon 24, so each row has its own origin at valid time minus 24 hours. They are not a common-origin
profile issued once for the entire next local day.

\section{Reconciliation}

For local day $d$ and hour $h$, the system uses:

\[
\hat{y}^{rec}_{d,h}
=
\hat{y}^{hourly}_{d,h}
\times
\frac{\hat{y}^{daily}_{d}}
{\frac{1}{|H_d|}\sum_{j\in H_d}\hat{y}^{hourly}_{d,j}}.
\]

The operation keeps the relative hourly shape. The reconciled mean equals the
daily forecast.

The code supports complete local days with 23, 24, or 25 hours. It rejects
invalid predictions and incomplete days.

Raw and reconciled MAE use the same forecast rows, so the difference isolates the daily constraint
rather than a change in sample. Reconciliation is performed before conformal scores are calculated.

\section{Model selection}

The release keeps the validation-selected model. Later candidate comparisons are descriptive and do
not change that selection.

The paired bootstrap resamples complete local days, preserving dependence among hourly errors within
a day. Its estimand is candidate minus reference MAE. A 95\% interval containing zero does not
establish a clear difference between the models.

\section{Prediction intervals}

The point, daily, and quantile models use data before calibration. The models stay fixed during
calibration and final evaluation. Symmetric split conformal intervals use frozen calibration
residuals. The adaptive variant updates its level sequentially with $\gamma=0.01$ and bounded
history. CQR estimates conditional lower and upper quantiles before conformal calibration.

Reconciliation occurs before score calculation. Both interval methods use the
same reconciled point forecasts.

Coverage, mean width, and interval score are considered together because coverage alone can be
increased by producing uninformatively wide ranges. Adaptive coverage is prequential monitoring
evidence and does not constitute an unconditional time-series guarantee under serial dependence.

The residual hybrid had reconciled validation MAE \HourlyValidationSelectedMAE{} MW, compared with
\HourlyValidationLightGBMMAE{} MW for direct LightGBM, and was fixed as the selected hourly model.

\subsection{Weather contribution and error structure}

Replacing forecast-origin weather with a previous-day persistence proxy modestly increased ensemble
MAPE. Weather adds measurable value, but the small change shows that recent and seasonal load
structure carries most of the signal at this national one-day horizon. Errors are not assumed
independent across hours: model comparison uses day blocks, while interval and drift diagnostics
retain monthly and horizon slices.

\section{Discussion}

Three conclusions follow from the experiment. A modest ensemble supplies an accurate daily anchor
without adding complexity after validation. The daily constraint improves the selected hourly
forecast while preserving its intraday shape. Finally, two hourly tree-based designs are too close
to justify claiming that either is generally superior. This negative result favors retaining the
validation choice instead of tuning against inspected outcomes.

The weather ablation qualifies the role of exogenous inputs. Forecast-origin weather improves error,
but lagged demand and calendar structure dominate at this scale. More spatial detail may matter
during regional extremes, yet testing that proposition requires regional load targets or a separate
aggregation study rather than simply adding stations.

\section{Limitations}

The evaluation period was inspected during development, so its numerical results describe this
release and are not prospective accuracy claims. The pre-2024 weather fallback is weaker than an
archived issue-time forecast. Five population-weighted cities cannot represent every regional
weather or industrial-load effect. SMARD revisions, market changes, and distribution shift can alter point
error and interval coverage.

The hourly headline is a fixed 24-hour-ahead evaluation, not a market-style profile issued once for
all hours of the next local day. The study concerns offline forecasting. It does not test data-arrival latency, scheduler
reliability, access control, or service-level objectives. Those are deployment questions and should
not be inferred from a retrospective model evaluation.

\section{Reproducibility}

The repository fixes split dates, feature definitions, parameters, and random seed in
\texttt{config.yaml}. A clean run downloads public inputs, rebuilds features, evaluates the frozen
protocols, compiles this report, validates artifact hashes, and emits the bounded dashboard payload.
Detailed CSV tables are generated on demand by the manual GitHub Actions workflow rather than stored
in Git. Source, protocol, and artifact hashes remain available in
\texttt{results/run\_summary.json} and \texttt{results/evaluation\_protocol.json}; they are omitted
from the scientific narrative because they support verification rather than interpretation.

Tree-model parameters are frozen values inherited from earlier experimentation. The complete
historical search trials are not available, so the study is reproducible from the committed
parameters onward but does not claim reproducibility of the original search.
